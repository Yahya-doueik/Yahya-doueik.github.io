No. 01 Sweet–Savory

Miso Butterscotch Noir

Umami-spiked caramel meets 70% dark chocolate in a truffle-dense no-bake slab — a completely untaken flavor territory that tastes like a Michelin-starred mignardise.

Yield
One 20×10 cm loaf · 10–12 slices
Chill Time
4 hrs min · Best overnight
Difficulty
Intermediate–Advanced
Ingredients
Flavor Journey
Roasted caramel→Umami salt wave→Dark bitter chocolate→Long butterscotch finish
Texture
Dense truffle-like fudge body with shattering shortbread pockets and a liquid butterscotch core that floods the palate on the bite.
Base
200g Biscoff cookies, broken into 1–2 cm irregular chunks (NOT crumbed)
40g unsalted butter, cooked to noisette (golden-brown stage)
1 tsp fleur de sel
Miso Dark Ganache
250g 70% dark chocolate, finely chopped
180ml double cream (35%+ fat)
35g white Shiro miso paste — never red or barley miso
50g unsalted butter, room temp, cubed
30g brown butter (cook 30g butter to golden, strain)
1 tbsp black treacle or molasses
1 tsp vanilla extract
2g fleur de sel (weigh this)
Butterscotch Core
80g caster sugar (dry caramel — no water)
30g unsalted butter
80ml double cream, warmed
½ tsp fleur de sel
¼ tsp white miso paste
Glaze & Garnish
120g 70% dark chocolate + 20g cocoa butter
1 tsp activated charcoal powder (optional — deepens color)
Gold leaf flakes (loose)
Fleur de sel — large pyramidal crystals
Black cocoa powder for dusting
Method6 Steps
01
FIRST: Freeze the Butterscotch Core
In a pale heavy-bottomed pan, cook caster sugar dry over medium heat. Swirl — never stir. Target 175°C deep mahogany amber. Off heat, add warm cream slowly (violent bubbling — normal). Stir in butter cubes, miso, salt. Miso makes this butterscotch taste deeper and more caramelized than standard. Pour into a narrow insert (16×6 cm, 1.5 cm deep) lined with cling film. Freeze 2+ hours until rock-solid. Unmold, trim, refreeze wrapped.
02
Make Brown Butter & Prepare the Base
Cook 30g butter in a small pan until milk solids turn golden and you smell toasted hazelnuts. Strain into a cool bowl. Cool to room temp. Separately melt 40g plain butter, mix with 1 tsp fleur de sel. Toss Biscoff chunks gently in salted butter — coated but not crushed. The irregular shard size is what creates the dramatic pockets in the finished cake. Refrigerate 20 min to firm.
03
Build the Miso Ganache
Heat double cream until just simmering — do not boil. Off heat, whisk miso into cream vigorously for 45–60 sec until fully dissolved. Pour miso cream over chopped chocolate in 3 pours, stirring center-out each time. Once glossy, add room-temp butter cubes one at a time. Add brown butter, treacle, vanilla, salt. Working temp: 28–30°C. The ganache should coat a spoon heavily and ribbon slowly.
04
Assemble — The Critical Step
Line 20×10×7 cm loaf tin with cling film, corners pressed, overhang on all sides. Pour 5mm base layer of ganache. Chill 10 min until tacky. Scatter half the Biscoff shards, press each halfway in. Cover with ganache. Working within 30 sec: remove frozen core from freezer. Center it, press to mid-depth. It must be perfectly level. Pour remaining ganache around and over the core, no air pockets. Add remaining biscuit shards on top. Tap tin 5–6 times to release air bubbles. Smooth top flat. Fold cling film over. Refrigerate minimum 4 hours, ideally overnight.
05
Black Mirror Glaze
Melt chocolate and cocoa butter together over bain-marie until smooth. Add charcoal powder if using. Cool to exactly 32°C — use a thermometer. Too hot (above 35°C): runs straight off. Too cold (below 29°C): sets lumpy. Unmold chilled cake onto wire rack over clean tray. Pour glaze from the center in one slow, confident spiral. Do not spread with a spatula — drag marks ruin the mirror effect. Let drip naturally. Set 5 min at room temp.
06
Garnish & Service
Scatter 8–10 large fleur de sel crystals. Place 3–4 pieces of gold leaf with dry tweezers — asymmetric, not symmetrical. Hold a fine sieve with black cocoa 30 cm above the edges and dust lightly. Rest 15–20 min at room temp before slicing — the butterscotch core needs to soften. Slice: heat knife under hot water, dry completely, single downward press, no sawing. Wipe blade clean between every slice.
StorageDome cover in fridge (not cling — damages mirror glaze). Flavor is deeper on Day 2. 3 days refrigerated, 4 weeks frozen unglazed.
Chef's Notes
Use only mild Japanese Shiro (white) miso — available at Asian grocery stores. Red and barley miso are too aggressive and will make the ganache taste savory rather than umami-complex. The white miso makes caramel taste more caramel than caramel itself. Do not skip the brown butter — it adds a roasted nutty dimension that bridges the miso and chocolate. The butterscotch core must be frozen solid before assembly or it will dissolve into the warm ganache.
No. 02 Global Fusion

Knafeh Royale

Lebanon's most iconic street dessert — kataifi, akkawi cheese, orange blossom syrup — rebuilt as a cold, sliceable no-bake luxury cake. All the ceremony, the crunch, the fragrance. Cold and elegant.

Yield
One 22×10 cm loaf · 10–12 slices
Chill Time
6 hrs min · Best at 12 hrs
Difficulty
Intermediate
Ingredients
Flavor Journey
Milky salt→Floral rose water→Sweet citrus syrup→Nutty pistachio
Texture
Cloud-soft white chocolate cheese cream over a crackling, syrup-soaked shredded-phyllo base. Every forkful delivers two wildly distinct textural acts.
Kataifi Base
200g kataifi (shredded phyllo) — fresh or fully thawed. Separate the strands before using
80g unsalted butter, clarified (melt, skim foam, use only the clear golden liquid)
½ tsp fresh orange zest
Pinch of fine salt
Orange Blossom Syrup
150g caster sugar
100ml water
2 tbsp orange blossom water — Lebanese Mazaher brand if possible
1 tbsp fresh lemon juice
1 tsp rose water — taste before using, it should smell floral not soapy
Akkawi Cheese Cream
300g Akkawi cheese — MUST be desalinated (see Step 1). Sub: low-moisture mozzarella
150g full-fat cream cheese, room temperature
200g Belgian white chocolate, finely chopped
100ml double cream
2 tbsp orange blossom water
1 tsp rose water
½ tsp vanilla extract
Garnish
80g raw pistachios — blitzed to a fine brilliant green crumb (not fine powder)
Edible dried rose petals — small and fragrant
Edible gold leaf (optional)
Few drops of orange blossom water for finishing
Method7 Steps
01
Desalinate Akkawi — Night Before or 3 Hours Ahead
Akkawi is packed in heavy brine. Without desalination it is inedibly salty. Cut into 1 cm slices. Submerge in cold water. Change the water every 45 minutes, 3–4 times total. After each change, taste a small piece. Target: milky, mild, barely-there background saltiness. If still salty: one more change. Low-moisture mozzarella substitute: skip this step, but pat very thoroughly dry — excess moisture breaks the cream.
02
Make the Orange Blossom Syrup
Combine sugar and water, stir to dissolve, bring to a boil — then stop stirring entirely (agitation causes crystallization). Cook 3 minutes until slightly viscous (a drop on a cold plate should be heavy, not a thread). Off heat: add lemon juice, orange blossom water, rose water. Stir once. Cool completely before use — hot syrup steams the kataifi and destroys the crunch.
03
Toast and Press the Kataifi Base
Preheat oven 180°C (fan 160°C). Separate kataifi strands with your fingers — it comes compressed and must be loosened. Toss with clarified butter, orange zest, salt until every strand glistens. Press firmly and evenly into the lined tin (22×10 cm, parchment base and sides). Target depth: 1.5–2 cm. Press very firmly with the flat base of a glass — denser compression = better crunch. Bake 20–25 min until deep golden caramel at the edges. Remove from oven. Immediately pour ¾ of cooled syrup over the hot base. It will hiss and absorb rapidly. Cool completely in tin — minimum 1 hour. Do not unmold.
04
Make the Akkawi White Chocolate Cream
Melt white chocolate with double cream in bain-marie. Never exceed 45°C with white chocolate — it seizes and cannot be recovered. Cool to room temp (20 min — do not skip). Beat desalinated akkawi and room-temp cream cheese 3–4 min until completely smooth, zero lumps. Scrape bowl. With mixer on low: drizzle in cooled white chocolate slowly. Add orange blossom water, rose water, vanilla. Medium speed 2 min until unified and holding a soft ribbon. Do not over-beat — you want a dense, pourable set cream, not whipped cream.
05
Assemble
Pour Akkawi cream over completely cooled kataifi base in the tin. Smooth patiently — the cream is thick. Press cling film directly onto the cream surface (touching it, not suspended). Refrigerate minimum 6 hours, ideally overnight. White chocolate sets the cream firm enough to unmold and slice cleanly.
ScienceWhite chocolate sets cream without eggs or gelatin because cocoa butter solidifies below 32°C. More white chocolate = cleaner, more stable set.
06
Unmold — Kataifi Side UP
Place your serving board directly on top of the tin. Invert in one confident motion. The kataifi base is now on top — this is the showpiece. Peel parchment carefully. Immediately drizzle the remaining ¼ of syrup evenly over the kataifi top. Scatter pistachio crumb generously across the entire surface — be bold. Add rose petals sparingly. Gold leaf using dry tweezers. Serve within 2 hours for maximum crunch.
07
Slice and Serve
Serrated knife: saw gently through the kataifi top, then press cleanly through the cream. Wipe blade between every cut. Serve on cold plates. Add 1–2 drops of orange blossom water directly on each plate at the table — the fragrance at close range is the final sensory touch.
Chef's Notes
The quality of orange blossom water is the single most important variable. Cheap brands taste chemical and synthetic. Buy Lebanese Mazaher, Palestinian Nabulsi, or any brand from a Middle Eastern grocery store. Kataifi is available fresh or frozen at all Middle Eastern grocery stores. Blitz the pistachio crumb fresh before use — pre-ground pistachios lose their vivid color and bright flavor within hours.
No. 03 Japanese Fusion

Tokyo Black Sesame Thundercloud

Jet-black, dramatically beautiful, electrically flavored. Black sesame paste has the depth of dark chocolate and the earthiness of peanut butter — fused with bright yuzu citrus, it becomes genuinely alarming in the best possible way.

Yield
One 20×10 cm loaf · 10–12 slices
Chill Time
4–5 hrs + 2 hrs for yuzu curd
Difficulty
Advanced
Ingredients
Flavor Journey
Deep roasted nut→Citrus lightning→Sweet cream→Lingering sesame
Texture
Velvet-dense jet-black fudge with crisp charcoal-coated cookie shards. A hidden yuzu curd ribbon cuts through the center with bright, jammy acidity mid-bite.
Charcoal Base
200g thin crisp sesame wafers or langue de chat biscuits
1 tsp activated charcoal powder (aesthetic only — no flavor)
30g black sesame paste (kurogoma)
40g unsalted butter, melted
Black Sesame Ganache
300g Belgian white chocolate, finely chopped
120ml double cream (35%+ fat)
80g black sesame paste (kurogoma) — stir the jar thoroughly before measuring
50g unsalted butter, room temp, cubed
2 tbsp yuzu juice (fresh or Kikkoman bottled)
1 tsp yuzu zest — grate frozen yuzu rind on a fine microplane
¼ tsp fine sea salt
Yuzu Curd Ribbon
3 large egg yolks
60g caster sugar
60ml yuzu juice
50g cold unsalted butter, small cubes
¼ tsp fine salt
Garnish
Edible gold leaf — 3–4 irregular pieces
2 tbsp white sesame seeds, toasted to deep gold
Fleur de sel — micro-pinch
Edible silver or gold lustre powder (dry brush application)
Optional: black sesame brittle (60g sesame + 80g sugar, caramelized)
Method7 Steps
01
FIRST: Make Yuzu Curd — 2+ Hours Ahead
Off heat: whisk egg yolks and sugar in a small pan until slightly pale. Add yuzu juice and salt. Place over medium-low heat. Stir constantly with a spatula, scraping base and corners — never stop. Takes 8–12 min to thicken. Do not increase heat to rush this — scrambled eggs are not the goal. Ready when it coats the spatula and a finger-drawn line holds clean edges. Off heat: add cold butter cubes one at a time, stirring vigorously after each. The cold butter cools the curd and stops the eggs cooking. Strain through a fine sieve. Cling film pressed directly on the surface. Refrigerate minimum 2 hours.
02
Build the Charcoal Base
Break wafers into irregular 1–2 cm shards by hand — some finer crumbs are fine. Whisk melted butter, black sesame paste, and charcoal powder until smooth and deeply dark. Toss biscuit shards in the black mixture until every piece is coated. Line loaf tin with cling film. Press coated shards into the base — distinct pockets, not a crumb-compressed mass. Refrigerate while making the ganache.
03
Make the Jet-Black Ganache
Heat cream until just simmering. Pour over white chocolate. Sit 2 min. Stir center-out. Once smooth, add black sesame paste — the mixture transforms from pale white to a deep charcoal, almost jet-black. This is the alchemy moment. Add room-temp butter cubes one at a time. Add yuzu juice, zest, salt. Yuzu may cause momentary graininess — keep stirring firmly for 60 sec and it re-emulsifies. Cool to 28–30°C.
04
Assemble — The Yuzu Ribbon
Pour half the ganache over the base. Smooth. Chill 20 min until just barely set (tacky but not liquid). Load the chilled yuzu curd into a piping bag. Pipe a single continuous ribbon down the absolute center length of the cake — 1 cm wide, full length, pressing the tip slightly into the ganache so it embeds. Every slice must intersect the ribbon. Pour remaining black ganache over and around the curd, covering it completely. Smooth top. Tap 5 times. Cover, chill 4–5 hours.
CriticalThe yuzu ribbon must run the full length and be centered precisely left-to-right. One millimeter off-center means some slices miss the ribbon entirely.
05
Mirror Surface Finish
For a glass-mirror surface: using a kitchen blowtorch at 15–20 cm distance, sweep the flame slowly and evenly across the unmolded cake surface. The top 1–2mm melts to a liquid gloss. Allow to re-set 2 min before garnishing. Work quickly — the window is about 5 min.
06
Garnish — The Sesame Halo
Hold a round cookie cutter 1 cm above (not touching) the cake surface as a stencil. Sprinkle toasted white sesame seeds to create a ring around the perimeter. Remove cutter. Place gold leaf at the center of the ring using dry tweezers. Scatter 4–5 large fleur de sel crystals outside the ring. Sweep edible silver lustre from 30 cm height with a very dry soft brush — light shimmer only, not a coating.
07
Slice — The Reveal
Heat knife in hot water, dry completely, single downward press. Each slice reveals: jet-black ganache body, sesame-coated biscuit shards, and a thin brilliant yellow yuzu ribbon running precisely through the center. The color contrast is dramatic. Serve at 12–14°C.
Chef's Notes
Black sesame paste (kurogoma) is available at Japanese grocery stores and Asian supermarkets. Do not substitute with tahini (different flavor profile) or sesame seeds blended at home (different texture). The yuzu curd can be made 3 days ahead. Bottled yuzu juice from Kikkoman or Clearspring works well. Emergency substitute: 50:50 fresh lime and mandarin — the floral note is lost but the brightness is there.
No. 04 MENA Fusion

Baklava Deconstructed

Every element of baklava — shatteringly thin filo, warm spice, floral honey, walnut, pistachio — rebuilt as a multi-layered cold sliceable cake. All the ceremony, none of the oven work at assembly time.

Yield
One 22×10 cm loaf · 12 slices
Chill Time
5–6 hrs · Tuile made ahead
Difficulty
Intermediate–Advanced
Ingredients
Flavor Journey
Warm spice bloom→Floral saffron honey→Nutty sesame tahini→Caramel brittle crack
Texture
Multi-layered complexity: silky-sticky ganache, brittle filo shards, chewy spiced nuts, glossy saffron glaze. Architecturally complex — each layer distinct in every bite.
Spiced Filo Shards
8 sheets filo pastry, fully thawed at room temp 2 hours
80g unsalted butter, clarified and melted
1 tsp ground cinnamon
¼ tsp ground clove (use carefully — very powerful)
¼ tsp ground green cardamom
60g walnuts, coarsely chopped (1–2 cm pieces)
60g pistachios, coarsely chopped
Tahini Spice Ganache
280g 40% milk chocolate, finely chopped
160ml double cream
60g raw tahini — must smell fresh and nutty
30g unsalted butter, room temp
1 tsp ground cinnamon + ½ tsp cardamom + ¼ tsp clove + pinch black pepper
1 tbsp good honey
Generous pinch fine salt
Saffron Honey Glaze
100ml good honey — orange blossom or sidr preferred
~30 saffron threads (0.1g if you have a precision scale)
2 tbsp hot (not boiling) water for infusion
1 tsp fresh lemon juice
Honey Caramel Tuile
100g caster sugar
40ml honey
20ml double cream
20g unsalted butter
Garnish
50g raw pistachios — fine brilliant green crumb
Gold leaf
3–4 sprigs micro mint or micro thyme
Fleur de sel — large crystals
Method7 Steps
01
FIRST: Make Saffron Honey Glaze
Crumble saffron threads into a small cup. Add hot (not boiling — boiling destroys volatile aromatics) water. Steep minimum 20 min — water should turn deep golden-orange. Combine infusion with honey and lemon juice. Warm gently 2 min — do not boil. Cool completely. Can be made 1 week ahead, stored in a sealed jar. Improves with resting time.
02
Bake the Spiced Filo Shards
Preheat 175°C (fan 155°C). Keep unused filo covered with a slightly damp towel — it dries and becomes brittle within 60 seconds of air exposure. Layer all 8 sheets on a tray, buttering and dusting with spice blend between each sheet. Cut the stack into irregular 3–5 cm pieces — organic shapes look more dramatic than neat squares. Bake 12–15 min until deeply golden amber (not pale gold — it must be amber or it will soften in the ganache). Immediately scatter chopped walnuts and pistachios over the hot filo and press gently — they adhere to the melted butter. Cool completely. Do not cover while warm — steam destroys crunch.
03
Make the Tahini Spice Ganache
Heat cream with all ground spices until just simmering. Remove, steep 5 min. Bring back to simmer — this double infusion extracts deeper resinous bottom notes. Strain through fine sieve over chopped milk chocolate, pressing firmly on the grounds. Sit 2 min. Stir center-out until glossy. Add tahini, butter, honey, salt. Stir until unified. Should smell powerfully of baklava already. Cool to 28°C.
04
Make Honey Caramel Tuile
Line baking sheet with silicone mat. Combine sugar, honey, cream in a small pan — cook over medium heat, stir until sugar dissolves, then stop stirring. Cook to 155°C hard crack stage. Off heat: add butter, stir quickly. Pour immediately onto mat in a thin, uneven layer — do not spread. Scatter fleur de sel. Cool completely until brittle (20–30 min). Break into dramatic irregular shards. Store at room temp — never refrigerate caramel tuile (moisture makes it sticky).
05
Assemble the Layered Cake
Line 22×10 cm tin with cling film. Base layer of ganache — 5mm. Chill 10 min until tacky. Layer: filo shards → ganache → nuts and filo → ganache. Repeat for 3–4 distinct layers total. Final top layer is ganache. Smooth surface. Drizzle 2 tbsp saffron honey glaze in a zigzag while the top ganache is still uncovered. Drag a toothpick through once in a slow S-curve — a hint of swirl, not a blended mess. Refrigerate 5–6 hours.
06
Unmold and Glaze
Unmold onto wire rack over a clean tray. Warm remaining saffron honey glaze until just fluid — not hot. Pour over the entire top in one even pour. Allow to run over the edges naturally. The glaze sets quickly on the cold cake — you have 3 minutes. Work without hesitation.
07
Final Garnish
Press pistachio crumb across the entire top while the glaze is still slightly tacky — cover the surface completely. Arrange caramel tuile shards architecturally: large pieces standing at angles, not lying flat. Add gold leaf. Add micro herb sprigs at one end only — do not scatter. Fleur de sel on the caramel shards. Serve within 4 hours for maximum filo crunch.
Chef's Notes
Filo shards must be baked to a deep amber — underbaked filo softens completely within the ganache and the textural point is lost. When in doubt, add 2 more minutes. Tahini brand fundamentally changes this recipe. Use Palestinian or Lebanese stone-ground tahini: Al Nakhil, Baroody, Al Arz, or Soom (available at Middle Eastern grocery stores). Processed supermarket tahini lacks the fresh, roasted complexity the recipe requires.
No. 05 Umami Luxury

Espresso Tonka Bomb

Tonka bean is the underground secret of haute pâtisserie — simultaneously vanilla, almond, tobacco, and wild cherry. Paired with cold brew and bitter amaretti, this is the most uncompromisingly adult dessert on this list.

Yield
One 20×10 cm loaf · 10–12 slices
Chill Time
4 hrs + 2 hrs for espresso core
Difficulty
Advanced
Ingredients
Flavor Journey
Cherry-tobacco tonka→Cold brew bitterness→Warm almond cookie→Dark cocoa fadeout
Texture
Dense truffle fudge. Cold-brew amaretti crumb pockets. Dry freeze-dried espresso powder explosion mid-bite. Then the snap of cold-tempered dark chocolate. Four distinct acts.
Cold Brew Amaretti Base
200g Italian amaretti cookies — HARD type (Lazzaroni), NOT the soft almond macaroon type
80ml cold brew concentrate or 4 shots espresso, cooled
20g unsalted butter, melted
NOTE: 3 seconds per side in the soak only — 60% saturation, retained crunch
Tonka Dark Ganache
300g 64% dark chocolate, very finely chopped
120ml double cream
60ml cold brew concentrate (added separately at the end — not in the cream)
1.5 tonka beans — grated on a fine microplane. They are extremely hard; use a good grater
30g unsalted butter, room temp, cubed
20g inverted sugar or light corn syrup (prevents crystallization during storage)
1 tsp vanilla extract
Generous pinch fleur de sel
Freeze-Dried Espresso Core
30g freeze-dried espresso powder — MUST be freeze-dried, NOT regular instant coffee granules
20g icing sugar
10g Dutch-process cocoa powder
5g tonka sugar: blitz 1 whole tonka bean with 50g caster sugar in a spice grinder, use 5g
Tempered Chocolate Snap Top
200g 70% couverture chocolate — Valrhona, Callebaut, or Michel Cluizel. Use couverture for proper tempering
NOTE: Tempering requires a thermometer. Non-negotiable.
Garnish
Tonka-cocoa dust: 15g Dutch-process cocoa + ¼ grated tonka bean
Fine amaretti crumble (30g amaretti processed to powder)
Fleur de sel — pyramidal crystals
Edible gold dust — single sweeping application
Method7 Steps
01
FIRST: Freeze the Espresso Core — 2+ Hours Ahead
Mix freeze-dried espresso powder, icing sugar, cocoa, and tonka sugar. Taste — intensely bitter, dry, concentrated. This is correct. Cut baking parchment to 10×16 cm. Spread the powder mixture in an even 3–4mm layer. Press into a uniform slab with a palette knife. Freeze on a flat plate for minimum 2 hours until rigid enough to handle. Work quickly when removing from freezer — you have ~60 seconds before it absorbs ambient humidity.
Why This WorksFreeze-dried coffee is processed at sub-zero temperatures, removing all moisture while concentrating aromatic compounds. When it contacts saliva it dissolves instantly, releasing a disproportionate blast of flavor. Regular instant coffee cannot substitute — it is neither dry enough nor concentrated enough.
02
Soak the Amaretti
Combine cold brew and melted butter in a shallow bowl. Working in batches of 3–4, dip each amaretti for exactly 3 seconds per side. Remove immediately. Do not linger. The cookie should be noticeably heavier and darker but retain its structure. Over-soaking = dissolved cookie = no crunch. Place in the cling film-lined tin in a flat single layer. Press gently so they lie flat. Refrigerate 20 min.
03
Make the Tonka Ganache
Heat cream until barely simmering. Off heat: grate exactly 1.5 tonka beans directly into warm cream using a fine Microplane. The aroma will be extraordinary: almond, vanilla, tobacco, cherry. Steep 5 min. Reheat to simmer. Pour over very finely chopped dark chocolate in 3 additions, stirring center-out after each. Once glossy, add cold brew concentrate (added now — not cooked — preserves its bright un-cooked flavor). Add butter, inverted sugar, vanilla, salt. Cool to 28°C.
04
Assemble — The Powder Bomb
Pour ganache over amaretti base to half the tin depth. Smooth. Chill 20 min until just barely set. Remove espresso slab from freezer. Working within 45 seconds: slide slab off parchment directly onto the set ganache, pressing gently to adhere. Pour remaining ganache immediately and evenly over the slab, covering it completely. No exposed powder. Smooth top. Tap 5 times. Refrigerate 4 hours minimum.
05
Temper the Chocolate Snap Top
Tempering requires a thermometer — do not attempt without one. Melt 150g of chocolate in a bain-marie to 50°C, stirring constantly. Off heat: add remaining 50g chopped chocolate (the seed) — stir until it melts. Monitor temperature. When it falls to 28°C, return to bain-marie briefly (5–10 sec at a time) to bring back to exactly 31–32°C. Test: dip a palette knife tip. Should set glossy and snap cleanly within 4 min. If it smears or blooms grey, re-melt to 50°C and start again.
06
Apply the Snap Top
Unmold the chilled cake. Pour tempered chocolate at exactly 31–32°C over the entire top surface — 3–4mm thick. Scatter fine amaretti crumble immediately on the wet chocolate. Add 5–6 fleur de sel crystals. Allow to set at cool room temp (18–20°C) — never refrigerate tempered chocolate while setting. Refrigeration causes condensation which blooms the surface white.
07
Dust, Slice, Serve
Sieve tonka-cocoa dust from 30 cm above the cake in slow sweeping passes — should look like dark earth coating. Sweep edible gold dust with a dry brush in one horizontal stroke. To serve: serrated knife to saw through the snap top (3–4 firm strokes), then press cleanly through the ganache below. Wipe blade. The cut face reveals the ganache body, amaretti crumb pockets, and a dark powder seam at the center cross-section. Serve immediately after slicing.
Chef's Notes
Tonka beans are available from specialty spice retailers. Use precisely 1.5 beans — more becomes medicinal. Freeze-dried espresso powder is available from specialty coffee suppliers: La Colombe, Sudden Coffee, and online retailers. Do not substitute regular instant coffee: particle size, moisture content, and concentration are completely different and the flavor explosion will not occur. Tonka beans store in an airtight container for up to 6 months.
No. 06 Spice Lab

Strawberry Szechuan Firecracker

Szechuan pepper produces a unique lip-numbing, tingling, electric sensation from the compound hydroxy-alpha-sanshool. Paired with fresh strawberry and white chocolate, it becomes a dessert that physically moves — alarming, then immediately craved again.

Yield
One 20×10 cm loaf · 10–12 slices
Chill Time
4 hrs
Difficulty
Intermediate
Ingredients
Flavor Journey
Sweet strawberry→Floral pepper bloom→Electric tingle→Fizzing pop→Sustained lip buzz
Texture
Creamy white fudge → crisp freeze-dried wafer pockets → popping candy explosions → slow Szechuan lip buzz lasting 10+ minutes. A dessert that happens TO you.
Freeze-Dried Strawberry Wafer Base
180g vanilla wafers (Nilla Wafers or thin vanilla biscuits)
20g freeze-dried strawberry powder
30g unsalted butter, melted
¼ tsp fine salt
Strawberry Szechuan White Ganache
320g Belgian white chocolate, finely chopped
120ml double cream
60g fresh strawberry purée: blend 100g fresh ripe strawberries, strain seeds
2 tsp Szechuan peppercorns — freshly toasted and freshly ground (never pre-ground)
30g unsalted butter, room temp
1 tsp fresh lemon juice
Pinch fine salt
Popping Candy — Handle with Care
40g popping candy — plain or strawberry flavor
CRITICAL: Add only at the final moment into ganache cooled below 35°C. Store in airtight container away from humidity until the exact moment of use. Heat and moisture permanently deactivate it.
Red Mirror Glaze
150g white chocolate, finely chopped
80ml sweetened condensed milk
80ml double cream
2 tsp powdered red food coloring or gel color dissolved in 1 tsp warm water
3g leaf gelatin (1.5 leaves), bloomed in cold water
Garnish
3–4 dehydrated or freeze-dried strawberry slices — whole rounds
Ground Szechuan pepper for atmospheric dusting
Halo of 10–12 whole Szechuan peppercorns, evenly spaced
Rose-gold edible lustre powder
White chocolate curls (optional)
Method7 Steps
01
Bloom the Szechuan Pepper — Test Your Peppercorns First
Test before committing: bite a single peppercorn and wait 10 seconds. You must feel a distinct tingling, buzzing sensation on your tongue and lips. If nothing happens within 20 seconds — your peppercorns are old, source a new batch. Toast peppercorns in a dry pan 45–60 sec, shaking constantly. Grind finely. Heat ground pepper with double cream to just simmering. Off heat, cover, steep 15 min. Strain through very fine sieve, pressing grounds. You should have ~100ml of Szechuan-infused cream that produces a faint tingle when tasted.
02
Make the Strawberry Purée
Blend 100g ripe fresh strawberries until smooth. Push through a fine sieve — press firmly to extract maximum liquid, discard seeds. Should yield ~60ml vivid red, intensely fragrant purée. Add lemon juice. If strawberries are not in season: use defrosted frozen strawberries — freeze-thaw ruptures cell walls and actually concentrates the flavor. Do not use jam, coulis, or sauce — added sugars and stabilizers change how the ganache sets.
03
Build the Strawberry Wafer Base
Mix melted butter, freeze-dried strawberry powder, and salt — creates a vivid pink-crimson paste. Toss vanilla wafers in the paste until every wafer turns a dramatic deep pink-red. Keep whole wafer pieces as intact as possible — this coating is part of the visual experience when sliced. Press loosely into the lined tin base. Refrigerate while making ganache.
04
Make Strawberry White Ganache
Melt white chocolate with Szechuan cream in bain-marie until smooth (keep below 45°C). Cool to 35°C. Add strawberry purée in a slow drizzle, stirring center-out. Momentary graininess is normal — the acid shocks the white chocolate emulsion. Keep stirring firmly for 60–90 sec and it re-emulsifies. Add butter, salt. Cool to below 35°C — test with wrist (should feel barely warm, not hot). This exact temperature is critical for the next step.
05
Add Popping Candy — 30-Second Window
The ganache MUST be at or below 35°C. Any warmer = candy pre-activates and dissolves during chilling, completely eliminating the effect. Open candy packaging. Tip directly into cooled ganache. Fold with one broad stroke only — do not stir repeatedly. You may hear faint crackling (some candy activating from contact moisture — acceptable). Pour the ganache immediately over the wafer base and smooth. Tap once. Refrigerate immediately. Every additional minute at room temp activates more candy.
06
Make Red Mirror Glaze
Bloom gelatin in cold water 5 min. Heat cream and condensed milk to just simmering. Off heat: add white chocolate, stir until melted. Add squeezed gelatin, dissolve completely. Add red coloring, stir until uniform. Strain. Cool to exactly 35°C — use a thermometer. Below 32°C: sets too fast. Above 38°C: runs off without coating. Have the unmolded cake on a wire rack ready before the glaze hits 35°C.
07
Glaze and Garnish
Pour 35°C glaze in one steady pour from the center. Let it flow and self-level — do not spread with a spatula. Set 8–10 min at room temp. Arrange dehydrated strawberry slices in a fan, pressing the base into the slightly soft glaze to anchor them standing upright. Create the peppercorn halo: space 10–12 whole Szechuan peppercorns evenly around the perimeter — signals the flavor, looks precise and intentional. Dust lightly with ground Szechuan pepper from 25 cm height. Rose-gold lustre sweep. Serve within 4–6 hours — popping candy remains active for approximately 6–8 hours.
Chef's Notes
The single most important variable is Szechuan peppercorn quality. Old or poor-quality peppercorns have no numbing effect. Buy from a Chinese grocery store — turnover is higher and freshness is far superior to supermarkets. The active compound (sanshool) degrades rapidly after grinding, so grind to order. Do not warn guests about the popping candy. The delayed realisation — usually about 3 seconds after swallowing — is the moment that makes everyone talk about this dessert.
No. 07 Aromatic Luxury

Smoked Cardamom Cloud

Cold-smoked cardamom meets burnt honey and milk chocolate in a campfire-scented, deeply aromatic cake that smells like a luxury candle and tastes profoundly better. A theatrical blowtorch moment at the table is the unforgettable service ritual.

Yield
One 20×10 cm loaf · 10–12 slices
Chill Time
3–4 hrs (mascarpone cloud made fresh at service)
Difficulty
Intermediate
Ingredients
Flavor Journey
Aromatic cardamom bloom→Smoky caramel→Milky chocolate→Salty cream finish
Texture
Smoky-crisp torched graham shards within a dense burnt honey fudge body. Topped at service with a cool mascarpone cloud torched to a golden crust. Dramatic hot-cold contrast.
Torched Graham Base
200g graham crackers or digestive biscuits — kept WHOLE for torching
50g unsalted butter, melted
½ tsp ground green cardamom (freshly ground from pods)
Pinch of fleur de sel
Kitchen blowtorch — essential, cannot be omitted
Burnt Honey
150g single-origin dark honey — buckwheat or chestnut preferred
NOTE: Use a pale-colored or stainless saucepan so you can monitor the color change precisely
Burnt Honey Cardamom Ganache
300g 40% milk chocolate, finely chopped
80ml double cream
80ml burnt honey (made in Step 1)
30g unsalted butter, room temp
1 tbsp green cardamom seeds (from pods), finely ground
¼ tsp fine sea salt
Optional: 1 tsp lapsang souchong tea steeped in the cream — adds campfire smoke depth
Smoked Salt Mascarpone Cloud — Made Fresh at Service
250g full-fat mascarpone, cold
30g icing sugar, sifted
½ tsp smoked sea salt (or ¼ tsp liquid smoke + ½ tsp regular salt)
½ tsp vanilla extract
1 tsp ground green cardamom
100ml double cream, very cold
Garnish
Crushed dried rose petals
Cardamom sugar: 30g caster sugar + ½ tsp ground cardamom
Smoked sea salt flakes
Dark honey for drizzling
Edible gold leaf
Method7 Steps
01
FIRST — Make Burnt Honey: The Most Critical Step
In a pale or stainless heavy-bottomed pan, pour honey over medium heat. Do not stir. Do not walk away. Honey goes through stages: large bubbling → settled simmer → color darkening. Flavor shifts from floral-sweet to sharp and bitter as you progress. When the honey turns very deep mahogany-brown and just begins to produce wisps of smoke, remove from heat immediately and pour into a heatproof glass bowl. Leaving it on heat for even 5 extra seconds after smoking = acrid and unusable. Burnt honey should taste bitter, sharp, complex, with a long caramelized finish. If it tastes just like warm honey — not cooked far enough.
02
Torch the Graham Crackers
Mix melted butter with ground cardamom and fleur de sel. Brush each whole graham cracker generously on both sides. Lay flat on a heat-safe surface (ceramic plate or metal tray). Using a kitchen blowtorch at 15–20 cm, sweep the flame steadily across each cracker in slow even passes. Target: golden to amber in the body, with darker caramelized spots at the thin edges. If the cracker smells of bitter char rather than sweet caramel — too far, use a fresh one. Cool completely until rigidly crisp. Break by hand into rough 3–5 cm pieces.
03
Build the Burnt Honey Ganache
If using lapsang souchong: heat cream with 1 tsp tea leaves to simmering, steep covered 10 min, strain. Heat cream with ground cardamom to just simmering. Pour over chopped milk chocolate. Sit 2 min. Stir center-out until glossy. Add burnt honey in a slow drizzle, stirring continuously — the bitterness of the honey dramatically cuts through the milk chocolate sweetness, creating a far more complex, grown-up ganache. Add butter, salt. Stir until unified. Cool to 28°C.
04
Assemble
Line loaf tin with cling film. Scatter torched graham pieces in the base in a roughly even single layer. Pour ganache over and around the pieces, palette knife to push ganache into gaps. Scatter a second layer of graham pieces on top, pressing in gently. Pour remaining ganache to fill. Smooth. Refrigerate 3–4 hours until firm-set but yielding — dense fudge-like consistency, not hard.
05
Make the Mascarpone Cloud — AT SERVICE TIME ONLY
Beat cold mascarpone with icing sugar, smoked salt, vanilla, and cardamom until smooth — 90 sec on medium. Add very cold cream. Beat to soft, billowy peaks — like a generously set whipped cream, not stiff peaks. Stop before it becomes grainy. Transfer to a piping bag with a large round tip. Refrigerate until the exact moment of service.
06
Torch Tableside — The Theater
Unmold and slice the chilled cake. Plate each slice. Pipe or quenelle a generous mound of mascarpone cloud directly on top of each slice. Dust immediately with cardamom sugar using a small sieve. Bring to the table. Use a kitchen blowtorch at 10–12 cm to sweep the flame slowly across the mascarpone surface. You are creating a crème brûlée-style golden crust. The exterior should blister, bubble, and turn amber. The interior remains cold. This is the moment that makes people remember this dessert.
07
Final Touches
Drizzle dark honey from a teaspoon at height over the torched mascarpone crust. Scatter 3–4 crushed rose petals on the plate (not the cake — on the plate around the base). Add smoked salt flakes to the crust. One piece of gold leaf using dry tweezers on the crest.
Chef's Notes
Burnt honey has an intensely complex flavor profile that cannot be approximated by any other ingredient. The transformation happens through Maillard reactions and pyrolysis that create hundreds of aromatic compounds not present in regular honey. Buckwheat honey from New York State or chestnut honey from Italy produce exceptional results. Lapsang souchong in the cream is optional but adds a campfire pine-smoke note that unifies all the smoky elements beautifully. One teaspoon is sufficient — more becomes overpowering.
No. 08 MENA Luxury

Cold Brew Tahini Halva Slab

Halva is one of the most texturally unique confections on earth — crystalline, dense, and melt-away — yet almost completely unexplored in Western luxury dessert contexts. This reframes it as the structural hero. The marble swirl requires zero skill and looks hyper-professional.

Yield
One 22×10 cm loaf · 12 thick slices
Chill Time
5 hrs · Praline up to 3 days ahead
Difficulty
Intermediate
Ingredients
Flavor Journey
Bitter sesame→Cold brew earthiness→Crystalline sweet halva→Pistachio salt explosion
Texture
Fudgy yet deliberately grainy from halva crystals. Pistachio praline provides explosive crunch. Dense, grown-up, zero sweetness fatigue. Addictive in small doses.
Halva Crumble Base
220g pistachio or plain sesame halva — from a Middle Eastern grocery, fresh block NOT pre-packaged supermarket halva
30g raw tahini
20g 70% dark chocolate, melted
Generous pinch fleur de sel
Tahini Cold Brew Dark Ganache
280g 70% dark chocolate, very finely chopped
100ml cold brew concentrate (or 4 strong espresso shots, room temp)
60ml double cream
100g raw tahini — well-stirred (oil layer fully incorporated before measuring)
40g unsalted butter, room temp, cubed
20g honey or date syrup (date syrup adds deeper complexity)
½ tsp fine sea salt
White Tahini Marble Swirl
80g Belgian white chocolate, melted and cooled to 35°C
40g raw tahini
Pinch fine salt
NOTE: This is swirled through the dark ganache to create an effortless marble pattern — keep at room temp until needed
Pistachio Praline Shatter
150g raw pistachio kernels, shelled and unsalted
120g caster sugar
40ml water
½ tsp fleur de sel
Edible gold dust for dusting the finished praline
Garnish
Pistachio crumb: 30g pistachios — medium-fine crumb
Halva crumble: 30g halva — rough pieces
Edible gold leaf — 3–4 pieces
Fleur de sel — pyramidal crystals
Tahini drizzle: 1 tbsp tahini thinned with 1 tsp honey to a fluid consistency
Method7 Steps
01
FIRST: Make Pistachio Praline — Up to 3 Days Ahead
Line a large baking sheet with a silicone mat. Toast raw pistachios in a dry pan 3–4 min until fragrant and lightly colored. Remove from pan, spread in a single layer on the silicone mat. In a heavy pan: combine sugar and water, cook without stirring over medium-high until the caramel turns deep amber (~160°C). Off heat: pour immediately over the spread pistachios in a thin, even stream. Immediately scatter fleur de sel. Do not touch — the caramel is extremely hot. Cool 30–40 min until fully brittle. Break into varying sizes: some fine crumble, some dramatic large shards. Store in an airtight container at room temp — never refrigerate praline.
02
Prepare the Halva Base
Break the halva block into irregular 1–2 cm pieces by hand — do not use a knife, which creates too-uniform pieces. The crystalline structure of fresh halva breaks naturally into organic, flaky shards. Mix melted dark chocolate, raw tahini, and fleur de sel until smooth. Toss halva pieces in this coating until each piece is lightly covered. Line tin with cling film. Press coated halva pieces into the base tightly — do not crush them into a uniform mass. Refrigerate 20 min to set the coating.
03
Make Tahini Cold Brew Dark Ganache
Heat double cream to just simmering. Pour over finely chopped dark chocolate. Sit 2 min. Stir center-out until smooth. While still warm (below 45°C): add cold brew in a slow steady drizzle, stirring continuously. Cold brew deepens the chocolate flavor dramatically — it makes chocolate taste more intensely of itself rather than tasting of coffee. This is the professional chocolatier technique of flavor amplification. Add raw tahini, stir until incorporated. Add butter, honey/date syrup, salt. Cool to 28°C.
04
Make White Tahini Marble
Combine melted 35°C white chocolate with raw tahini and a pinch of salt. Stir until smooth and pourable. Keep at room temp — do not refrigerate or it will solidify before you can swirl it. The difference in viscosity between this and the dark ganache is what creates defined marble ribbons rather than a muddy blended color.
05
Assemble and Marble
Pour ¾ of dark tahini ganache over the halva base. Smooth level. Now pour the white tahini marble in an irregular pattern: 3–4 continuous lines running the length of the cake, then 2 lines crossing perpendicular. Using a toothpick or thin skewer, drag through the ganache in 6–8 long, sweeping S-curve motions — stop after 8 drags. Over-dragging blends the colors into a muddy grey. Pour remaining dark ganache over the top in a thin layer to just cover the marble pattern. Smooth. Refrigerate 5 hours.
06
Apply Praline and Garnish
Unmold onto a chilled serving board. Press a generous mixed layer of pistachio praline across the entire top — a combination of large dramatic shards and fine crumble. Press shards in gently so they adhere. Scatter halva crumble in the gaps. Press pistachio crumb around the base of the praline. Drizzle tahini-honey mixture from a spoon at height in slow sweeping arcs. Add gold leaf with dry tweezers. Scatter fleur de sel on the praline shards.
07
The Marble Reveal
To serve: heat knife in hot water, dry completely, single clean downward press through the praline layer (firm and direct), then through the ganache below. The cut face reveals white tahini ribbons swirling through deep brown ganache, with crystalline halva chunks interrupting throughout. No two slices will have the same marble pattern. Photograph every cross-section.
Chef's Notes
Fresh halva from a Middle Eastern grocery store is fundamentally different from pre-packaged supermarket halva. Fresh halva has a flaky, crystalline, melt-away texture created by millions of tiny sesame-oil and sugar crystals. Pre-packaged halva is often compressed and stale. Raw tahini is made from unroasted sesame seeds — more complex and fresher-tasting than regular tahini. Both are available at Middle Eastern grocery stores. Cold brew coffee functions as a flavor amplifier here rather than a coffee flavoring — at this quantity in dark chocolate ganache, it makes the chocolate taste more intensely of itself.
No. 09 Textural Madness

Passion Fruit Feuilletine Tropicale

Feuilletine is the ingredient that separates professional chocolatiers from home bakers — caramelized crêpe dentelle flakes that stay impossibly crisp inside chocolate ganache for days. Paired with fresh passion fruit and a frozen gelée core, this is the most technically refined cake on the list.

Yield
One 20×10 cm loaf · 10–12 slices
Chill Time
5–6 hrs total (gelée core 3 hrs separate)
Difficulty
Advanced
Ingredients
Flavor Journey
Bright tart passion fruit→Sweet coconut cream→Vanilla white chocolate→Lime zest lift
Texture
Feuilletine flakes that shatter without effort → cloud-soft tropical ganache → cold wobbly gelée core contrasting the room-temp ganache → slight chew of toasted coconut. The lightest, most elegant cake on the list.
Feuilletine Coconut Base
150g feuilletine flakes — Gavottes brand crushed crêpes dentelles, or from specialty baking suppliers
100g white chocolate, melted and cooled to 35°C
40g refined coconut oil (refined = no coconut flavor — use refined specifically)
20g desiccated coconut, toasted to deep gold in a dry pan
Pinch fine salt
Passion Fruit Coconut Ganache
280g Belgian white chocolate, finely chopped
100ml coconut cream — the thick solid from the top of a refrigerated full-fat can
80ml fresh passion fruit pulp: ~6–7 passion fruits, seeds fully strained
30ml fresh lime juice
1 tsp fresh lime zest — grate directly into the ganache
25g unsalted butter, room temp
¼ tsp fine sea salt
¼ tsp vanilla extract
Passion Fruit Gelée Core
120ml fresh passion fruit juice, fully strained
30ml fresh lime juice
20g caster sugar
3g leaf gelatin (1.5 leaves), bloomed in cold water
NOTE: Dimensions of the gelée slab must be narrower than the main tin: 14×6 cm, 1.5 cm deep
Garnish
50g desiccated coconut — toasted to deep golden amber (watch extremely carefully, it burns in seconds)
2–3 fresh passion fruit halves for presentation
Fresh lime zest curls — made with a channel zester
Edible tropical flowers or micro-orchids
White chocolate curls: draw a vegetable peeler across a room-temp block of white chocolate
Method7 Steps
01
FIRST: Make and Freeze the Passion Fruit Gelée Core — 3+ Hours Ahead
Bloom gelatin in cold water 5 min. Heat passion fruit juice, lime juice, and sugar over low heat — stir to dissolve sugar, do not boil. Boiling degrades the aromatic top notes. Off heat: squeeze excess water from gelatin and add to warm juice, stir to dissolve completely. If lumps remain — warm very briefly and stir again. Strain. Pour into a small container lined with cling film — dimensions must be narrower and shorter than the main loaf tin (14×6 cm, 1.5 cm deep) so that when embedded in the cake, ganache surrounds it on all sides. Freeze minimum 3 hours until completely solid. Unmold, rewrap in cling film, return to freezer.
02
Make the Feuilletine Base
Mix melted, cooled white chocolate with refined coconut oil until completely fluid. Add toasted coconut and a pinch of salt. Fold in feuilletine flakes using a gentle, broad folding motion — not stirring, which breaks the flakes to dust. Preserve maximum shard size. The white chocolate-coconut oil creates a hydrophobic (water-repelling) barrier around each flake, keeping them crisp for days inside the ganache. Line the loaf tin with cling film. Press feuilletine mixture into base in an even 1.5 cm layer — compressed but not crushed to paste. Refrigerate 20 min.
Why Feuilletine Stays CrispCocoa butter from white chocolate creates a hydrophobic barrier around each flake, preventing moisture in the ganache from softening it. This is the professional chocolatier's technique for maintaining crunch in fat-based confections.
03
Make the Passion Fruit Coconut Ganache
Melt white chocolate with coconut cream in bain-marie, keeping below 45°C. Smooth. Cool to 35°C. Add passion fruit pulp in 3 additions. After each addition the ganache may seize or look grainy — the acidity destabilizes white chocolate momentarily. Stir firmly for 60–90 sec and the emulsion restores. Add lime juice, lime zest (grated directly into the bowl for maximum essential oil release), room-temp butter, salt, vanilla. The ganache should be pale gold-yellow and smell vividly tropical. Cool to 28°C.
04
Assemble with the Frozen Gelée Core — Speed Matters
Pour exactly half the passion fruit ganache over the feuilletine base. Smooth. Refrigerate 15–20 min until just barely set (tacky not flowing). Remove frozen gelée slab from freezer. Working within 30 seconds: center the frozen slab on the set ganache, pressing firmly and evenly until it embeds slightly. Longer exposure before ganache contact = condensation on the gelée surface = moisture barrier preventing adhesion. Pour remaining ganache immediately around and over the embedded slab, covering it completely. Smooth top. Refrigerate 5–6 hours.
05
Toast the Coconut Garnish
Spread desiccated coconut across a dry baking tray in a layer no thicker than 3mm. Bake at 160°C for 6–9 min, checking every 2 min and stirring for even color. The window between perfectly toasted and burnt is very narrow — stand at the oven. Target: deep golden amber, not pale gold, not brown. Transfer immediately to a cold plate to arrest cooking. Cool completely — warm coconut is sticky and will clump.
06
Unmold and Garnish
Unmold onto a chilled serving board. Press deeply toasted coconut across the entire top surface generously — like thatching a roof, not scattering a garnish. Cut or halve 2–3 fresh passion fruits and press them cut-side-up into the coconut layer at one end. The vibrant orange, seed-filled face of a fresh passion fruit against golden coconut is one of the best natural color moments in dessert. Add lime zest curls. Tropical flowers at one end — asymmetric. White chocolate curls.
07
Service — The Gelée Reveal
Remove from fridge 10–15 min before slicing. At this temperature the frozen core transitions to soft-set — still cold and slightly firm, but yielding and wobbly. The thermal contrast between room-approaching ganache and still-cold gelée is at its maximum at this exact moment. Slice with a warm, dry knife in a single press. Each slice reveals: pale golden ganache body, golden feuilletine base, and a horizontal band of translucent passion fruit gelée running through the center. The gelée layer may be slightly trembling when plated — this visual quality is part of the experience.
Chef's Notes
Feuilletine is available from specialty baking suppliers including Valrhona and Cacao Barry distributors, and online. In a pinch, very finely crushed Gavottes crêpes dentelles (a French biscuit sold in specialty food shops) produce an identical result — they are in fact the source product. The passion fruit gelée core is irreplaceable — do not substitute with a curd. The wobbling, cold, translucent layer inside warm ganache is a thermal and textural experience with no substitute. The coconut oil must be refined (not virgin) — virgin coconut oil adds a competing coconut flavor.
No. 10 East–West Fusion

Hojicha Roasted Tiramisu

Tiramisu is one of the most beloved desserts on earth. Hojicha is Japan's roasted green tea — smokier, darker, and infinitely more complex than matcha. This collision produces a no-bake cake that makes classic tiramisu feel safe and timid by comparison.

Yield
One 22×10 cm loaf · 12 slices
Chill Time
5 hrs min · Best overnight
Difficulty
Easy–Intermediate
Ingredients
Flavor Journey
Roasted tea smokiness→Creamy mascarpone→White chocolate sweetness→Dark rum depth→Caramel drizzle
Texture
Cloud-light, sliceable mascarpone body with a distinct ladyfinger layer. Velvety, sophisticated. The hojicha dust coats the palate in warm roasted tea that lingers beautifully.
Hojicha Ladyfinger Base
24–28 Savoiardi ladyfingers — Italian style, the dry slightly firm type. NOT the soft French type
250ml water, heated to 90°C
4 tbsp hojicha tea leaves OR 2 tsp hojicha powder for the concentrate
1 tbsp caster sugar, dissolved in the warm concentrate
30ml dark rum — strongly recommended. Alternatively: 1 tbsp rum extract
Hojicha White Chocolate Mascarpone
500g full-fat mascarpone, room temperature — cold mascarpone causes lumps that do not dissolve
200g Belgian white chocolate, melted and cooled to exactly 30°C
2 tbsp hojicha powder — must smell smoky and roasted, not green or vegetal
1 tsp vanilla extract
Pinch fine sea salt
Optional: 1 tbsp hojicha liqueur or dark rum
Dark Caramel Drizzle
80g caster sugar
30ml double cream, warmed
15g unsalted butter
¼ tsp fleur de sel
¼ tsp hojicha powder — ties the caramel to the overall flavor profile
Garnish
4–5 tbsp hojicha powder — for a generous, defining top dust
Edible gold dust — swept with a dry soft brush
Dark caramel drizzle (from above)
4–5 whole toasted hojicha tea leaves for visual contrast
Optional: small piece of crystallized ginger
Method7 Steps
01
Make the Hojicha Concentrate
Heat water to exactly 90°C — boiling water over-extracts hojicha and produces harshness. If you don't have a temperature-controlled kettle: boil and let cool 3 min. Add hojicha leaves or powder. Steep exactly 4 minutes. Strain through fine sieve. Add sugar, stir to dissolve. Add dark rum while still warm. Cool to room temp — do not use warm concentrate on ladyfingers, as heat causes them to collapse rather than soak. Taste test: dip a dry ladyfinger 3 seconds — it should be noticeably and deeply flavored, not just tinged. If it tastes weak: simmer the concentrate 5 min to reduce and intensify.
02
Make the Hojicha White Chocolate Mascarpone
Melt white chocolate in bain-marie until smooth. Cool to exactly 30°C — non-negotiable. Any warmer = melts the mascarpone into soup. Beat room-temp mascarpone alone for 60 sec until perfectly smooth and lump-free. Any lumps now will remain in the final cream. Add cooled white chocolate in 3 additions, folding gently after each. The white chocolate both flavors and sets the cream — it is the structural element that allows this to slice cleanly without eggs or gelatin. Add hojicha powder, vanilla, salt. Fold until uniform — a warm caramel-amber color. Should hold a soft ribbon from the spatula.
03
Make the Dark Caramel Drizzle
Cook caster sugar in a small pan over medium heat without stirring. Swirl gently as it melts. When it reaches deep amber (175°C), off heat. Add warm cream carefully — intense bubbling. Stir in butter, fleur de sel, and hojicha powder. The hojicha blooms in the hot caramel and releases its roasted aroma — smell it at this moment. Cool until thick but still fluid (~30 min). Transfer to a piping bag or squeeze bottle.
04
Soak the Ladyfingers — Precision Determines the Cake
Working in batches of 3–4: dip each ladyfinger in the hojicha-rum concentrate for 3 seconds per side. Flip. 3 seconds. Remove immediately. Ladyfinger should be noticeably heavier and darker, but its structure intact. Over-soaking (5+ sec per side) = sodden collapsing cookie that does not slice cleanly. Under-soaking (less than 2 sec) = dry, flavorless interior.
Test FirstBefore soaking all your ladyfingers, test one. 3 sec per side, then cut in half. Exterior should be dark and wet; the very center just slightly drier and lighter. This is the correct saturation for a sliceable cake.
05
Assemble the Tiramisu Cake
Line 22×10 cm tin with cling film. Lay soaked ladyfingers in a tight, seamless single layer — cut them as needed to fill the width completely with no gaps. They form an unbroken floor. Press very gently. Pour half the mascarpone cream over the first layer. Smooth patiently from edges inward. Add a second layer of soaked ladyfingers, pressing very gently so they make even contact with the cream below. Pour remaining cream over the second layer. Smooth top completely flat. Drizzle half the dark caramel in a free-hand pattern across the smooth cream surface. Drag a toothpick through in slow S-curves — 3–4 light strokes only. Press cling film directly onto the surface. Refrigerate minimum 5 hours, ideally overnight.
06
Unmold
Lift the cake from the tin using the cling film overhang. Place on your serving board. Peel cling film slowly and evenly. The top will be smooth and pale caramel-amber with the caramel swirl pattern visible. The cake should feel firm but yielding when pressed gently — like a dense mousse. If it feels very soft or slumps at the edges: return to the fridge for 1 more hour.
07
Dust with Hojicha — The Defining Transformation
Load a fine-mesh sieve generously with hojicha powder. Hold at 30–40 cm above the cake. In slow, confident sweeping passes, dust the entire top and slightly over the sides. Make the dusting thick — far thicker than you would dust cocoa over standard tiramisu. Hojicha is lighter in color than cocoa and needs volume for visual impact. The top must be completely and evenly coated. Apply edible gold dust with a very dry soft brush in 2–3 sweeping horizontal strokes across the hojicha surface — the gold against the dark brown is the signature visual. Drizzle remaining caramel in fine threads from height. Place 4–5 whole hojicha tea leaves at one end. Slice with a sharp chef's knife — no warming needed. Each slice reveals: amber cream body with two distinct horizontal bands of soaked ladyfinger visible throughout the full width of the slice.
Chef's Notes
Hojicha powder is a fundamentally different product from matcha. It is roasted bancha or kukicha tea ground to a fine powder — roasting produces pyrazine compounds responsible for its smoky, toasty, caramelized aroma. If your hojicha powder smells green, grassy, or mild, it is not good quality. Source from a Japanese tea specialist: Ippodo, Palais des Thés, or Yunomi. The white chocolate set mechanism makes this superior to classic egg-based tiramisu in several ways: safer (no raw eggs), more stable (4 days refrigerated versus 2 for egg-based), and produces a cleaner cross-section. The flavor deepens significantly between Day 1 and Day 2 as the hojicha infuses throughout the cream.
